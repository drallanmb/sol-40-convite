Append a plain CSS block to the end of `src/index.css`, after the existing base rules. Do not touch the `@theme` block: Phase 1's tokens are consumed by every other component and a stray edit there is a project-wide regression.

Define two keyframe sets. `wave-scroll` translates a band horizontally from zero to minus fifty percent, which is why each wave path must be drawn twice end-to-end inside its SVG — the halfway point of a doubled tile is visually identical to the start, so a linear infinite loop never shows a seam. `light-shimmer` oscillates opacity between 0.85 and 1 and back.

Define the four classes the component applies. `.wave-band` runs `wave-scroll` over 22s, linear, infinite. `.wave-band--mid` overrides the duration to 30s and `.wave-band--back` to 38s — the stagger is what produces parallax rather than three bands sliding as one sheet. `.golden-light` runs `light-shimmer` over 3.5s with an ease-in-out timing function, infinite.

Then add a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` block that sets `animation: none` on all four classes. This is the hard requirement from D-08 and it has a specific shape: the elements keep their normal opacity, fill and transform — they stay on screen as static art at their rest position. The reduced-motion block must switch off motion and nothing else. Do not resolve it by removing the art from the render in any way: not by taking the elements out of layout, not by collapsing them, not by fading them away to nothing. Removing the sunset is not an accessible alternative to animating it, and the block should contain no property other than `animation`.

Create `src/components/invite/SeaWaves.tsx` exporting a `SeaWaves` component with no props. It renders the sea layer that sits under the horizon line inside the hero, absolutely positioned and `aria-hidden="true"`.

Layer one is the waves: three stacked SVG elements, each drawn with a `preserveAspectRatio` that lets it stretch across the full hero width, each containing a wave-crest path drawn twice side by side (the doubled tile the keyframe depends on). Apply `.wave-band` to all three and add `.wave-band--mid` to the middle and `.wave-band--back` to the rearmost. Make the front band the most opaque and the rear the least, and offset their vertical positions slightly so they read as depth. Use the sea and plum tones from the design system — the water sits in the dark part of the sky gradient, so the bands should be dark blues shading toward the horizon rather than bright colour.

Layer two is the golden light path: a soft vertical trapezoid running from directly under the sun down to the bottom edge of the hero, filled with a vertical gradient from a warm sun tone at the top fading to transparent at the bottom, with soft edges rather than a hard-edged beam — use a blur filter or a horizontally-fading gradient mask, not a crisp polygon. Apply `.golden-light` to it. Place it above the wave bands in stacking order so the shimmer reads as light on the water.

Import nothing beyond React. The whole component is markup plus class names; all motion lives in the stylesheet.
